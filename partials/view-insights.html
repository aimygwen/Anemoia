Log · Identity

8 Little Bit of Nostalgia

Holo-stickers, acrylic glare, and the clicky click snap of arcade sounds.

Just Vibing.

My style is a love letter to that era. In a world where modern visuals constantly drift into generic, overly sterile corporate looks, I found myself running full speed in the opposite direction.

I wanted to bring back the fun—that heartwarming, playful energy where every pixel felt like it had a soul. By pairing clean, minimal visual layouts with a retro vibe I just can't let go of, I'm building a space where nostalgic hardware aesthetics meet crisp, modern design.

Glitter, Gloss & A Touch of Chaos

My visual identity needed to feel like a physical artifact—something you could hold, collect, or slap onto a retro console.

The mascot is the heart of my persona. Finding its shape meant wading through a mess of early sketches, balancing crisp minimalism with genuine warmth.

After endless iteration, it clicked: a high-shine mascot inspired by custom acrylic keychains, laced with subtle holo-glitter. Clean, minimal, and full of character.

The Charm

Look closer

The header mark softens away — then the same face settles here, large enough to read every lash and ear tip. Keep scrolling and it holds; scroll back up and it rises with you.

To balance the clean geometry of the Charm, the “Aimy Gwen” Wordmark goes full Y2K. Styled like a custom holo-sticker with bubbly Powerpuff Girls-esque proportions, it injects pure, nostalgic playfulness into my world.

The Persona

At the center of all these low-poly worlds, glossy keychains, and nostalgic visual logs is my character. She isn't just a mascot or a design exercise—she's the literal avatar of my creative brain, bringing a human heart to these digital spaces.

Whether she's hanging out inside a pixelated 32-bit menu, rendered as a physical charm, or floating across a pastel backdrop, she embodies the exact mood I want my art to give off: playful, cozy, and unapologetically retro. She is the anchor that connects the physical merch aesthetics to the virtual worlds I build, turning cold polygons into a home.

Log · Workspace

My desk and how I work

The physical desk, the digital one, and the rituals that keep messy work from turning generic.

The loop

Work moves in loops — sketch, blockout, light, doubt, revise. Some days are forests and crossroads; others are copper pipes and soft toys. The desk is where the loop stays visible: references pinned, variants named honestly, nothing hidden in a folder called final_final.

Tools, not talismans

Blender for meshes, Affinity for texture passes, a plain notes file for decisions I will forget by Thursday. The stack is boring on purpose — generic is the enemy, but gear worship is its own kind of generic.

Log · Hytale

Models & UV structure

How Hytale assets get blocked, unwrapped, and packed so they stay readable in-game — not just in a render.

Blockout first

Silhouette before polish. I keep face counts honest early so proportion reads at gameplay distance — if the shape only works in a turntable, it does not ship. Extension sets share a visual dialect: rounded corners, readable material breaks, no noise for noise's sake.

UV mapping

UVs are layout design. Islands get grouped by material story, not by what was convenient in edit mode. Texel density is matched across a set so a furnishing does not glow sharper than its neighbor. Seams hide in creases and undersides — places the camera and the player both forgive.

Structure & variants

Variant swaps live on predictable islands so recolors do not reshuffle the whole sheet. Naming follows the pack, not my mood. When a mesh needs nine colorways, the UV plan is decided before the first texture pass — otherwise every variant becomes a small emergency.
