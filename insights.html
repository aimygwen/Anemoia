Atmosphere & Light

A walk through the quieter side of the work — how sketches become meshes, how light finds a room, and how a world gathers itself before anyone else sees it. Four chapters. One messy, careful practice.

Generic is the enemy — every mesh keeps a secret.
The Con cept

The Idea

Behind the Madness is not a changelog — it’s the residue of making: the wrong palette kept overnight, the camera angle that finally felt honest, the low-poly silhouette that refused to look generic. Insights gather those residues into something you can sit with.

The Process

Work moves in loops — sketch, blockout, light, doubt, revise. Some days are forests and crossroads; others are copper pipes and soft toys. The process is the point: staying close to the feeling until the frame holds it.

Chap– ters

  1. Chapter 1

    The Sketch

    Everything starts loose — graphite, color blocks, a silhouette that might become a creature or a hallway. The sketch is permission to be wrong in public with yourself.

  2. Chapter 2

    The Mesh

    Low-poly is a language of edges. Faces are counted, materials are chosen, and the form has to hold character with almost nothing — like a joke told in five words.

  3. Chapter 3

    The Light

    Atmosphere does the emotional work. Warm spill, cool shadow, a window that feels like dusk. Light is how a quiet world starts speaking.

  4. Chapter 4

    The Share

    Then it leaves the desk — a pack, a still, a scrap of process. Sharing is the last edit: what stays private, what becomes a note for someone else in the middle of making.

Brand– ing

The Mark

The Aimy mark is a small face that has to work as a signal — readable in a tab, loud enough in a corner, quiet enough not to bully the work. Hair first. Softness second. Eyes last, and darkest.

The Palette

Primary is the hairstyle — the obvious brand color. Secondary is the pastel face, bow, and base. Tertiary sits in the irises and lashes so the gaze still cuts through. Accent pink stays for UI, not for the logo layers.

The Logo

Look closer

The header mark softens away — then the same face settles here, large enough to read every lash and ear tip. Keep scrolling and it holds; scroll back up and it rises with you.

Notes

Making is music before it’s a finished piece. Each chapter leaves a residue — a habit, a palette, a refusal — that becomes the next world.
